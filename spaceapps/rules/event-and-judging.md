## 2. What a submission must contain (project page fields)
Source: Submission Guide. The project page is what judges review ("the majority of judges' evaluation will be based on your project submission"); the demo counts toward overall evaluation and can raise the Presentation score.
1. **Project Name**
2. **High-Level Project Summary**: what was developed, how it addresses the challenge, why it matters.
3. **Link to Project Demo**: a slide deck of **max 7 slides including title and intro slides** OR a video of **30 seconds or less**. Hosted externally (YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox), public link, no login/permission/registration. Exceeding 7 slides / 30 s means the project is "not eligible for Global Judging". Deck must show team name, team member names, the challenge selected; visuals should show an image of the project, the user experience, and how NASA/space agency data and technology make the solution possible.
4. **Link to Final Project**: public link to a website, hosted app or code repository (GitHub etc.), no login. A repository link is an accepted form; the app/repo must be reachable by judges. Optional: request a FortiGuard web filter classification for a newly created website so judges' networks do not block it (steps in the Submission Guide).
5. **Detailed Project Description**: what it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools/languages/hardware/software used.
6. **Use of "Super Intelligence" (SI)** (the official term for AI): whether and how SI tools were used. This answer is for data collection and is not shown to judges (watermarks/metadata are).
7. **NASA Data**: specific NASA data and resources used.
8. **Space Agency Partner Data and Other References**: list ALL code, text, images, data, tools used, even open source or free ones.
- Images can be uploaded to the page (JPG/PNG/GIF up to 10 MB); videos and documents cannot, they must be linked.
- Required checkboxes at submit: Terms and Conditions; "Confirm All Work is Original" (original work, all external resources listed in References, no copyrighted material used without permission). All required fields must be complete or Submit is disabled. Unlimited resubmits until the deadline; only the last saved submission is judged.
- Language: English for the form and all linked materials. A video demo may be spoken in another language only with English subtitles; slide decks must be English.
- Max length of text fields: **not found** (guide gives no character limits).
- The 5-minute live pitch is a local-event format; **not found** in global documents.

## 3. Judging criteria (scored 1-5 each, at every level: Local, Universal, Global, Executive)
Source: Judging & Awards Guide. Five criteria:
- **Impact**: addresses a minor or major problem; breadth and quality of effect; how much it inspires or helps others. 5 = significant problem, extensive reach, "could change the world".
- **Creativity**: originality and innovation; a novel concept or a meaningful improvement over existing ideas. 5 = groundbreaking, never attempted.
- **Validity**: grounded in scientific principles; technical/functional/logical feasibility; completeness; **usability and user-friendliness** count. 5 = scientifically sound, fully developed, highly usable.
- **Relevance**: fully and directly addresses the challenge AND meaningfully integrates NASA data "as a core component of the proposed solution rather than a superficial addition". 5 = NASA data integral and well executed.
- **Presentation**: clarity about challenge, solution and significance; storytelling, organization, delivery. "Judges evaluate the demo and full project to compute this score." The guide urges equal attention to Presentation and recruiting storytelling/design skills.
(inference) Relevance needs real NASA data/resources inside the product (instrument/launcher/orbit parameters sourced from NASA documents, real-mission comparisons like the Webb game, GMAT-derived values), not only a citation. Validity includes UX, so a working, clear UI matters.

## 4. Judging path and awards
- Phase 1: Local Event judges (recruited by the Local Lead) pick **Global Nominees** using the same criteria. Local awards are optional and separate from global. "Only officially submitted Global Nominees will be considered for Global Judging." Nominees per event: **not found**.
- Phase 2: expert Global Judges rate nominees; top teams become Global Finalists (Dec 17). Honorable Mentions (ranked highly for ingenuity/creativity) do not advance to Executive Judging or awards.
- Phase 3: executive committee from NASA and Space Agency Partners nominates teams for ten Global Awards: Best Use of Science; Best Use of Data; Best Use of Technology; Galactic Impact; **Best Mission Concept** ("most plausible concept and design"); Most Inspirational; Best Storytelling; Global Connection; Art & Technology; Local Impact.
  (inference) Most natural targets for this challenge: Best Mission Concept, Best Storytelling, Best Use of Data, Art & Technology.
- Eligibility for any Global Award requires using at least one NASA data/resource (Submission Guide, "NASA Data").
- All participants who submit by the deadline get a certificate; the guide stresses the event is "collaborative, not competitive."

## 5. Rules that constrain us
- **Eligibility for global judging** (Judging Guide, FAQ): respond to an official challenge; all team members registered, same Local Event, not waitlisted, listed on the team's Members tab (no additions after submission closes); Local Event teams need at least 1 person; **max 6 members**; one team per person, one challenge per team. (The challenge text's "up to four" refers to players in the game, not our team size.)
- **Under-18s**: demos and final projects "may not include any images, video, audio, or other likeness to persons under 18 years old" per the Terms (Submission Guide). Keep faces/voices/photos of minors out of slides, video and repo assets.
- **Work before the event**: FAQ: "Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon." Plus the originality checkbox. List reused libraries/assets in References; do not present pre-event code as new. How generic pre-existing boilerplate is treated: **not found**.
- **SI/AI tools** (FAQ + Submission Guide): permitted "to accelerate innovation and creativity" but "should not be used to come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge." Disclosure required. Generated images/videos need a **visible watermark**; generated audio, code and data must be acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata. Judges can see these indicators and assess originality, intent, execution. (inference) Add an SI-disclosure section in the README and mark AI-generated code/data files in headers or metadata.
- **NASA branding**: SI-generated content "may not use, contain, or modify any NASA branding or design elements (such as the NASA logos, flags, or mission identifiers)" (Submission Guide). NASA Brand Center (via search results; page text not directly fetched): Insignia ("meatball"), Logotype ("worm") and Seal require explicit permission, are not to be used as branding/advertising on third-party sites, and may not appear with AI-generated imagery. Sources: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines , https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/nasa-logo-use/ . Older Space Apps Navigator terms: do not imply NASA authorization or endorsement, https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/navigator-terms-conditions/ . (inference) Do not use the meatball/worm in the game or deck; use own branding; a text credit such as "uses NASA data/resources" is fine. Official Space Apps logo usage: Brand Guidelines page is JS-rendered, text **not found**.
- **Licensing / open source**: search excerpts of the 2026 Participant Terms report that solutions must be freely and publicly available or at minimum OSI-licensed open source, and that NASA may remove nominations/awards from teams that do not cite resources (https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/ ; full text not read, JS-rendered). Treat as likely, verify. (inference) Public repo with an OSI license (MIT/Apache-2.0).
- **Third-party assets**: all code/text/images (even open source) listed in References; copyrighted material only with permission (Submission Guide; originality checkbox in Judging Guide). Check licenses of music, fonts, images, 3D models.
- **China funding restriction**: teams are reviewed for prohibited bilateral activity with China or Chinese-owned entities for global eligibility (Judging Guide). Relevant only if a member/affiliation falls under it.
- Space Agency Partner data is "highly encouraged"; 2026 partners include CSA, ESA, JAXA, ISRO, UK Space Agency and others (Submission Guide). This challenge's partner resource is the CSA digital games page.
